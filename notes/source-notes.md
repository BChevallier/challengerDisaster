# Challenger — source notes (raw material, not a draft)

Page numbers: Rogers = printed page in Vol. 1 (PDF page ≈ printed + 7). Vaughan = chapter.

## The joint (the engineering object)
- SRB built from stacked steel segments joined by tang-and-clevis "field joints", sealed by two Viton O-rings (primary + secondary) plus asbestos-filled zinc-chromate putty. Rogers p.121–122.
- Derived from the Titan III (one O-ring, very reliable), but Titan's O-ring was "able but not intended" to take combustion pressure; the Shuttle's O-rings were *designed* to take it. Longer tang (to fit 2 rings) → bends more. Reusable up to 20 times. Rogers p.121–122; Vaughan ch.3 ("In reality, the SRB joints were a different, and untried, design.")
- Contract (1973): cost was decisive; NASA praised the dual-O-ring joint as "innovative". Rogers p.120.

## The deviation, step by step
- 1977 hydroburst test: joint **opens** at ignition instead of closing ("joint rotation"), up to ~0.052 in. at the primary O-ring. Thiokol: not significant. Rogers p.122–123.
- 1977–79 Marshall engineer Leon Ray (memos signed by John Miller): "no change" = "unacceptable"; "mandatory to prevent hot gas leaks and resulting catastrophic failure". O-ring vendor: ring asked to work "beyond its intended design". Rogers p.123–124.
- 1980: joint classified Criticality **1R** (redundant) — while the same document says secondary redundancy "cannot be verified" after ~40% of max pressure. Rogers p.125.
- Nov 1981, STS-2: first in-flight erosion (0.053 in.), worst field-joint erosion ever recovered — not reported at the next Level I review. Rogers p.125.
- Dec 1982: reclassified **Criticality 1** (single point failure; "Loss of mission, vehicle and crew"). Waived March 1983, days before STS-6. People kept calling it 1R afterwards. Rogers p.126–128.
- 1984 (STS 41-B): erosion now treated with a calculated "safety margin": max expected erosion 0.090 in., lab seal holds at 0.095 in. → "not a constraint to future launches." Rogers p.128–129.
- Leak-check pressure raised 50→100→200 psi to make sure the putty didn't mask a bad seal; it blew holes in the putty. Field-joint anomalies went from 1 in 9 flights to >half; nozzle anomalies 12% → 56% → 88%. Known and accepted. Rogers p.133–134 (Fig. 3).
- Jan 1985, STS 51-C at 53 °F (coldest yet): blow-by, first heat damage to a *secondary* O-ring; Boisjoly: grease "jet black". Thiokol: "condition is not desirable but is acceptable". Rogers p.135–136.
- 1985 bench test (Thiokol): at 100 °F ring keeps contact; at 75 °F loses contact for 2.4 s; at 50 °F does not regain contact in 10 min. Rogers p.137.
- April 1985, STS 51-B: nozzle primary eroded 0.171 in. (model's max was 0.090) and never sealed; secondary eroded. Launch constraint imposed July 1985 — then waived for every flight up to and including 51-L. Rogers p.137–138.
- Aug 1985 HQ briefing: "lack of a good secondary seal in the field joint is most critical". Rogers p.139.
- Rogers findings: they "accepted escalating risk apparently because they 'got away with it last time'"; Feynman: "a kind of Russian roulette". Rogers p.148.

## The concept (Vaughan)
- Normalization of deviance: evidence first read as a deviation from expected performance is reinterpreted, step by step, as within acceptable risk. Ch.4.
- Five-step sequence, repeated flight after flight: (1) signal of potential danger → (2) official act acknowledging escalated risk → (3) review of evidence → (4) official act indicating normalization of deviance: accepting risk → (5) shuttle launch. Ch.3–5.
- By end of 1984 a three-part technical rationale: "safety margin", "experience base", "self-limiting" erosion. Ch.4.
- "Acceptable risk" was a formal NASA status, not a euphemism; the list of acceptable risks before STS-1 filled six volumes. Ch.3.
- "No fundamental decision was made at NASA to do evil; rather, a series of seemingly harmless decisions were made that incrementally moved the space agency toward a catastrophic outcome." Ch.10.
- Delta review / "management by exception": only *changes* went up the hierarchy, so a recurring problem covered by an old rationale disappeared from upper reviews. Ch.3; Rogers finding 4 p.148.
- Columbia 2003 repeated the pattern (foam strikes) — Vaughan's new preface.
